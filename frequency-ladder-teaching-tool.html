How it all connects — notes

▶Frequency is a rotation ratemath

A frequency f counts repetitions per second. The cleanest picture of "repeating" is a point going around a circle — a phasor. Angle is phase, revolutions per second is frequency, and the point's height traced over time is a sine wave. Everything else in this tool is that one rotation, dressed differently.

The rotation is shown at its true rate — 1 Hz is one revolution per second. Above a few hertz a 60 fps display can only strobe the motion (the wagon-wheel effect), just as a camera would. The Time-dilation slider is an explicit, labelled slow-motion control when you want to inspect the cycle.

▶What the FFT really doesmath

The FFT asks a wave: which rotation rates are hiding in you? Winding the waveform around a circle at a trial rate leaves the drawing balanced — unless the rate matches a component, when the graph piles up on one side and its centre of mass jumps away from zero. Each jump is a spectrum bar.

The bars here come from a genuine radix-2 FFT of the sampled wave (1024 samples, one exact period), so the spectrum recovers exactly the harmonic recipe you dialed in — nothing is echoed back by shortcut.

▶Mandalas are spectra in polar spacemath

Read the spectrum in polar coordinates: harmonic n with amplitude aₙ becomes an n-petalled ripple, r(θ) = R₀ + Σ aₙ·cos(nθ + φₙ). Fold-symmetry is frequency ratio, drawn in space; phases rotate the petals. A mandala is a spectrum you can look at.

▶The horn torus closes the loopmath

A horn torus is a torus whose tube radius equals its ring radius, so the hole pinches to a single point — the vortex. Signal flow lives on its surface: the fundamental carries particles around the axis (toroidal), harmonic n wraps them n times through the pinch per lap (poloidal), tracing a (1,n) winding.

Seen from above, the windings are the mandala's rose curves. A particle's height over time is the waveform. The FFT of that trace is the spectrum. One motion, four shadows — that is the ladder.

▶Solfeggio & mod-9: lore, labeledlore

The solfeggio frequency chips (174–963 Hz) and the mod-9 mandala overlay come from numerology culture, not acoustics — the same "3-6-9" vortex-math family covered (and carefully fenced off) in this repo's Rodin-coil math notes. Digit sums of multiples repeat with period 9 because we write numbers in base 10; that is real arithmetic, but it appears nowhere in the physics of waves.

They're included because the patterns are pretty and the boundary between math and lore is itself worth teaching.
