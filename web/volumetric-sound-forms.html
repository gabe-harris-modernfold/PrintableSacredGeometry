A tool in two plates · acoustics & the printable solid

Volumetric
Sound Forms

Give the page a recording and it draws what every audio editor draws: the waveform, the height of the sound against time. Then it turns that picture on a lathe. Each window of the clip becomes a thin disc as wide as that moment is loud and as thick as its pitch is low, threaded on a spindle, so a spoken word comes out as a stack of fins that swells where the voice is strong and thins to a needle where it falls silent. What comes out is a solid you can hold, sized for a 320 mm printer, watertight by construction.

That is the plainest reading of a sound, and the page offers two more. The spectral cylinder stands the spectrogram up: time runs up a column, pitch runs around it, and the loudness of every band raises a ridge on the wall. The cavity form is closer to what sound is in a room. Inside a vessel a frequency is not a stripe but a standing wave, pressure that swells in lobes and falls away at nodal surfaces where the air hardly moves; the clip is played, in the arithmetic, by a small speaker in the wall of a cone-shaped vessel, and the loudest air becomes solid, fused to the vessel's shell.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. The decoding, the transform, the mesh and the STL are all computed in this tab, and the sound never leaves it. A synthetic spoken “ah”, 2.4 seconds long, is loaded at the start so the plates have something to show, and a pure 100 Hz tone is a button away; replace either with your own file with the button above, or drop the file onto the plate.

The waveform solid

A waveform turned on a lathe

  1. decoded The browser's own codec turns the file into samples at 44.1 kHz; stereo is folded to mono, and the trim under More controls keeps the stretch you want: one word makes a better sculpture than a sentence.
  2. measured The kept stretch is cut into as many equal windows as there are fins. A window's height is its peak, the largest swing of the waveform inside it, which is exactly what an audio editor draws; rms takes the root-mean-square instead, smoother and nearer to loudness. Heights are divided by the loudest window and raised to the curve: below 1 lifts the quiet parts, above 1 sharpens the loud ones.
  3. pitched Each window's pitch is found by YIN: the waveform is compared with itself shifted by every period from 1/1200 to 1/50 of a second, and the first shift at which it nearly repeats is its period. A window that never repeats (a hiss, a breath, near silence) has no pitch of its own and borrows its neighbours'. With thickness by pitch a disc is as thick as its period is long, as a wavelength is: the clip's middle pitch gets the middle thickness, an octave lower doubles it and an octave higher halves it, held between the fin thickness and four fifths of the spacing so a gap always stays open.
  4. outlined Each window becomes a fin centred in it: a disc of that thickness at the rim (fin millimetres when thickness is fixed), standing above the spindle by the window's height times the radius. Its flanks lean by the flare angle, widening toward the root until it meets its neighbours, so the fins are stiffest where they join and no two points of the outline share a place on the axis. A window quieter than a tenth of a millimetre leaves bare spindle, and the ends close in cones, the tips.
  5. turned The outline is spun about the axis in 160 facets. Each ring is joined to the next by a band of quads and the ends by fans to the poles, so every edge is shared by exactly two triangles and the body is closed before any check runs. Its volume is checked against the exact volume of that stack of faceted frusta, to a part in a hundred thousand.
  6. laid down Whole, the solid lies along its axis on its largest disc, as it is shown. As halves it is cut through the axis and both halves lie on their flat cut faces side by side; every half-disc then stands upright on the bed, no face leans past vertical, and it prints without supports. Glue the halves face to face.

Waveform profile drops the windows: the radius follows the peak at every 0.3 mm along the axis, so the outline is the waveform itself at that resolution. Over a whole clip it reads as a dense, bristling envelope; trim to a fraction of a second and the single cycles of the voice appear, each a ridge of its own.

How the spectral cylinder is made

From pressure to polygon in six moves

  1. decoded The browser's own codec turns the mp3 or wav into samples at 44.1 kHz; stereo is folded to mono.
  2. transformed The trimmed clip is split into as many moments as the time steps control asks for. Each moment is a Hann-windowed frame of 1024–4096 samples, sent through a fast Fourier transform. When the moments are far apart, several frames are averaged per moment so nothing between them is skipped.
  3. banded The bins are gathered into bands, spaced logarithmically (every octave the same width, as the ear hears) or linearly. Power in each band is summed, converted to decibels against the loudest band in the clip, and clipped at the floor. Gamma and contrast shape the curve; blur smooths along the axis and around the wall.
  4. laid out The band table becomes the wall. Mirror runs the bands up one side and back down the other so the surface meets itself without a seam. Plain runs them once around and accepts a seam where the highest band meets the lowest. Time around turns the table: the whole clip wraps the circumference, low bands at the foot, high at the rim.
  5. raised Each cell of the wall sits at radius R₀ + relief × loudness. Rows are joined to their neighbours by two triangles each, the ends are closed by fans from the axis, and because every edge is shared by exactly two triangles the body is closed and orientable before any check is run. Hollow bodies add an inner wall, offset inward by the wall thickness after a minimum filter so the shell never thins on a slope, closed by a floor and a flat rim.
  6. written The triangles are streamed into binary STL, 50 bytes each, in millimetres, and the manifold cell reports the edge count, the Euler characteristic and the signed volume the slicer will see.

The cavity form

The shape of the air in a ringing vessel

A rigid vessel has its own ways of ringing: its modes. Across any level slice each mode is a Bessel pattern, Jm(κρ) cos mθ, with m nodal lines through the axis and rings of nodes around it, the pressure always at a maximum against the rigid wall. Up the height each mode is a standing wave between the floor, which is rigid, and the top: rigid under a lid, and at an open mouth a plane where the pressure falls to zero. For a cone the slice pattern is carried up the height by the slowly-varying-duct equation −(a²Z′)′ + κ²Z = k²a²Z (Webster's horn equation when κ = 0), solved in finite volumes for every mode with k·a₀ ≤ 22: some seven hundred of them for the default vessel.

The clip drives them as a loudspeaker in the wall would. Each mode answers the sound's power near its own frequency through a resonance of quality Q, in proportion to how strongly the speaker touches it; a mode's answer to a given push weakens as the square of its frequency, so its share of the energy falls as the fourth power, and the low modes lead. The time-averaged pressure adds the modes' energies; the cross terms between modes are dropped, which is exact where their resonances do not overlap and a fair average where they do.

Tune sets the scale. The frequency you choose lands on the first sideways mode of the widest cross-section, and the vessel then holds the band from its lowest mode up to about twelve times that frequency, some three and a half octaves; Plate I shows the band, with every mode ticked at its frequency. A real vessel 50 mm in radius rings its first sideways mode near 2 kHz, so tuning is the plain admission that the print is a scale model of a vessel large enough to sing your sound.

The air has no surface, so the page chooses one: fill makes the loudest share of the cavity's air solid, 15 % by default. In moment mode the fill follows how hard that instant sets the vessel ringing against the clip's average, so a hush leaves the vessel nearly empty and a strike fills it. The lobes and a shell of the vessel are one implicit function, the larger of the two at every voxel, meshed by marching tetrahedra: closed and consistently wound by construction. A lobe that touches neither wall nor floor could not print; it is removed and counted. A pocket of quiet air closed in by loud lobes is kept as a sealed void and reported. The manifold cell gives the genus, and a lobe that bridges the cavity shows there as a handle.

checked from outside

The slowly-varying-duct equation is exact for a straight cylinder and an approximation for a cone. The repository solves the same frustum again by finite elements, without the approximation: the two agree within 0.8 % in frequency for the default wall, which leans 4.8° from vertical, within 1.2 % at 9.5°, and within about 2 % at 14°; the Analysis cell flags anything steeper. A numpy re-implementation rebuilds the modes with LAPACK and the field with exact Bessel functions, and VTK's marching cubes rebuilds the surface: they agree with this page to a part in a million in the field and a fraction of a percent in volume.

physics the modes, their shapes and frequencies, the resonance. model rigid walls, a point speaker, the dropped cross terms, the open mouth as a plane of zero pressure. choice the threshold that says where the print is loud.

Printing notes

What the printer will and will not forgive

The waveform solid is a stack of thin discs, and a disc lying on its side is an overhang: whole, the lower half of every fin hangs in air. Print it as halves. Each half lies on its flat cut face, every half-disc stands upright like a page in a rack, and the Overhang cell reads 0°. Keep the fin at three nozzle widths or more (1.2 mm for a 0.4 mm nozzle; with thickness by pitch it is the thinnest disc, the highest note) and the core at 3 mm or more for a 240 mm piece; the flare thickens the roots where a fin would snap. Glue the halves face to face.

A loud transient next to a quiet one is a step in the wall. If the relief is deep and the time steps are many, that step can lean past 45° and the printer will droop into it. The cures are the ones on the plate: lower the relief, or under More controls raise the blur along the axis, use fewer time steps, or set Overhang to 45°, which fills a cone under every ledge so that no face leans further, at the cost of a little plastic; the Overhang cell reports the steepest face either way. The plain foot gives the slicer a flat first layer to grip.

A solid body prints as a solid, or as a spiral vase if your slicer offers that mode: it keeps only the outer wall and climbs it in one continuous line, which suits this shape well. Choose hollow instead when you want a real vessel with a floor and a chosen wall, and keep the wall at or above two nozzle widths.

With fit bed on, a body that would overhang the limit is scaled down uniformly and the scale is reported in the bed cell; wall and floor scale with it. Turn it off to print the numbers exactly as set and read the extents yourself. Files above a million triangles are flagged and above two million are refused, not because the geometry is wrong but because no slicer thanks you for a hundred-megabyte STL.

The cavity form is a vessel with lobes on its inner walls, and lobes are overhangs. Whole, it prints upright with supports from the floor; tree supports reach inside. As halves, the vessel is cut through its axis and both halves lie on their flat cut faces, which gives the first layer the whole cut to grip, with supports under the vault; flip them in the slicer if you would rather print the inside facing up. A closed lid seals the cavity inside the body, so print a closed vessel as halves to see anything. Finer voxels give smoother lobes and larger files; the wall is never thinner than one and a half voxels, and the voxel size rises by itself past four million voxels.

Where the seam falls

measured, then chosen

The spectrogram is measurement: the same one an engineer would read, computed the same way. The cylinder is a choice. Time need not climb; pitch need not turn; the mapping is a convention this page adopts because it makes a vessel, and vessels are pleasant to print and hold. Nothing here claims that this is the shape of the sound. It is one honest projection of it, and the plate says which.

tradition The Hermetic reading arrives on its own: the column as time rising, the round as the recurring, the loud moment cast permanently outward from the axis it came from. Take it or leave it. The triangles are the same either way.

Colophon

Zero dependencies, by house rule: the Fourier transform, the lathe, the mesh builder, the manifold check, the STL writer, the Bessel functions, the mode solver, marching tetrahedra and the 3D view are hand-written in this one file. A numpy re-implementation in the repository regenerates the same demo and compares fields and vertices against this page's output; scipy, LAPACK, VTK and a scikit-fem finite-element model check the cavity form from outside. Colour follows the mineral-lattices plates, so a form you tune here sits beside them without argument.
