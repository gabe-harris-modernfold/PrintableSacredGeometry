The Hermetic Phenomenon Profile

a structured way to look at anything — and stay honest about how you know what you know

Pick a phenomenon — a crystal, a candle flame, a garden, a machine, a friendship. This instrument walks it through the seven principles of the Kybalion, used here as lenses rather than beliefs, and produces one integrated portrait: its physics, its patterns, its cycles, its causes — and its symbolism, kept in a clearly marked lane of its own.

The house rule, same as every study on this site: what is measured is marked measured, and what is symbolic is marked symbolic — the two never blend.

The exposé · how to use it, in plain words

How to read anything like a careful Hermetic scientist

The whole method fits in six moves. A quartz crystal rides along as the running example — the full, rigorous version of every move is in the sheets below.

Draw a line around your subject

Decide what is the thing and what is just its surroundings, and write that down. This is the system boundary, and it quietly controls every later answer: what counts as a cause, what counts as an outside push.

Quartz: the crystal is the thing; the shelf, the room, and the sunlight are surroundings.

Pick your window

Choose how long you will watch and how closely — the observation window. Watching for one second and watching for one year give different portraits, and both can be true. Say which one you made.

Quartz: five days on a bench, at hand scale, with a thermometer logging the room.

Learn the six letters

Every single statement — every claim — gets one letter that says how you know it: M you measured it, C you computed it, I you deduced it from clues, R someone else reported it, A it is a symbol or comparison, U you don't know yet. The letters are an honesty system, not a grading system — an A is welcome, it just can't pretend to be an M.

Quartz: "21.4 °C on the thermometer" is M. "Feels calm and clear" is A. Both go in the profile — in different lanes.

Ask the seven questions

Walk the wheel. Vibration: what shakes, rings, or glows, and how fast? Polarity: what slider between opposites is it sitting on? Correspondence: what pattern repeats between its smallest parts and its whole shape? Rhythm: what cycles does it ride, and how far does it swing each way? Cause & Effect: what pushes it, through what mechanism, with what result? Gender: what two different roles couple to make something neither makes alone? Mentalism: and how do you actually know all of the above — which instruments, which assumptions?

Quartz: it rings at exact frequencies; squeezing it makes voltage; its inner atomic grid repeats as its outer faces; the room's day-night temperature rocks it; squeeze-in causes charge-out; stress + lattice together create a signal; and we know all this through three instruments we can name.

Count your letters

When the sheets are full, tally the letters. Mostly M C? You have a physical portrait. Mostly A? You have a symbolic portrait — also fine, as long as the final write-up says so. Every U becomes a to-do: what to measure next, and with what.

Write the one-paragraph portrait

Fill the synthesis template at the bottom of the page. Every sentence keeps its letter, the unknowns get named out loud, and the last line says — honestly — which register the whole portrait speaks from.

The six letters · one confidence vocabulary

Claim-type labels

Solid chips are first-hand, outlined chips are second-hand, the italic chip is symbolic, the dashed chip is honest ignorance. Form and colour both carry the meaning.

MMeasured

You, or your instrument, directly observed it. The strongest kind of claim.

"Surface temperature 21.4 °C (thermocouple)"
CCalculated

Math done on measurements. As trustworthy as the numbers and the formula it came from — both of which it must cite.

"Main flex tone ≈ 3.2 kHz, from the measured size and stiffness"
IInferred

A detective's conclusion from clues. Must name the clues and the reasoning that connects them.

"The stain pattern implies past water exposure"
RReported

Someone else measured it — a book, a datasheet, a witness. Must name the source; becomes M only after you verify it yourself.

"d₁₁ ≈ 2.3 pC/N for quartz (resonator literature)"
AAnalogical

A symbol, comparison, or meaning-level reading. Welcome in the profile — and never evidence for a physical claim.

"Reads as a still point in an agitated room"
UUnknown

You don't know yet — and say so. Every U spawns a measurement-plan row: what to measure, with what, at what resolution.

"Damping ratio: U → measurement plan"
Label rules
  1. One label per field. A statement that mixes registers gets split into two labeled statements.
  2. The register firewall. An A never serves as evidence or mechanism for an M, C, I, or R claim. What a symbol may do is inspire a hypothesis — which enters the sheet as a U with a proposed measurement. No amount of symbolic resonance ever adds up to a physical frequency.
  3. Citation duties. Every C cites its measurements and formula; every I cites its clues and the model that licenses the leap; every R names its source.
  4. The weakest-link rule. A conclusion inherits the shakiest label in its support chain: built on an I, it is at most an I; resting on any A, it is an A.
  5. U is an action, not a shrug. A profile with unlisted unknowns is incomplete, not cautious.
  6. Labels survive to the end. A synthesis sentence without its letter is invalid.

Default labels: cross-domain analogies (Sheet III) and Gender's role-readings (Sheet VI) default to A unless upgraded by a cited structural or mathematical basis. Mentalism's instrument fields (Sheet VII) are M/I claims about the measuring chain, not about the object.

SHEET I

Vibration — the spectral identity

“Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates.”Kybalion ch. IX
What periodic, spectral, oscillatory, or wave-like behavior characterizes it?

Record a whole spectral identity, not one “frequency.” Mechanical resonance, electrical oscillation, light absorption, thermal motion, and visible color are separate phenomena — tag each entry with its physical domain. Slow, state-level cycles belong on Sheet IV; the boundary rule lives there.

  • Mechanical fundamental frequency: Hz  
  • Harmonics / dominant mode shapes:  
  • Acoustic emission or response: dB SPL at Hz  
  • EM emission / absorption features: Hz or nm  
  • Perceived color:   (observer report; illuminant: )
  • Spectral character: reflectance / transmittance / emission spectrum; dominant wavelength nm where one exists   — magenta and the other purples have none, white and gray leave it undefined; brown, by contrast, is low-brightness orange and does have one
  • Thermal state and variability: °C or K ±  
  • Resonances: center frequency , bandwidth , Q , damping ratio  
  • Character: ☐ stable  ☐ broadband  ☐ periodic  ☐ pulsed  ☐ chaotic  ☐ damped  ☐ resonant  

Consistency check: the resonance numbers are linked — Q equals the center frequency divided by the bandwidth, and for ringy resonators the damping ratio is roughly 1 divided by twice the Q. Record whichever you actually measured M, derive the others C, and make sure the filled-in values agree with each other.

Guardrail · the category error of this lens

Color is not “the object's vibration frequency.” Color is the mix of light reaching your eye — bounced off, passed through, or glowing from the object — and it is a different physical fact from the object's mechanical or electrical resonances. Never merge them into one number.

Strong entry“Fundamental flexural mode 440 Hz, Q ≈ 1 200, laser vibrometry” M
Weak entry“Vibrates at a high frequency” — no domain, no number, no label.
SHEET II

Polarity — axes with poles

“Everything is dual… opposites are identical in nature, but different in degree.”Kybalion ch. X
What measurable or conceptual axis has opposed poles?

Admission test. An axis carrying M or C claims names one measurable quantity, signed or graded, whose values span both poles around an identifiable neutral point. That is the axiom made operational. The test gates which axes may carry measured claims — not which may appear: a conceptual or relational axis with no measurable quantity (in a relationship, an organization, a ritual) may still be recorded, labeled A, or I with cited evidence.

  • Primary axis: which polarity most governs the phenomenon?  
  • Neutral point: where is the balance, zero crossing, equilibrium, or inversion?  
  • Transformability: what input moves the state along the axis?   — this field is the operational shadow of the Kybalion's “transmutation,” movement along the axis of degree A
  • Type of opposite: a true physical counterpart, an absence, or a complementary process? (Negative charge is a counterpart-pole; cold is an absence-pole of heat.)  
Guardrails · the category errors of this lens

Categories are not poles. “Crystal vs. non-crystal” is a classification, not a slider — unless recast as one (degree of crystallinity, 0–100%).

Sign is not goodness. The plus or minus on a physical quantity carries no moral or wellness charge: negative charge is not “negative energy.” If such a reading is kept at all, it is an A.

Strong entry“Piezoelectric charge separation ± under axial stress, zero at rest” M
Weak entry“It has positive energy” — no axis, no quantity, no counterpart pole.
SHEET III

Correspondence — the pattern across scales

“As above, so below; as below, so above.”Kybalion ch. VIII
What patterns recur across scales or domains?
  • Micro: what happens at molecular / cellular / grain / component scale?  
  • Meso: what geometry or organization shows at object scale?  
  • Macro: what larger system contains or shapes it?  
  • Repeated relation: does the same relation — not merely a similar look — recur across scales?  
  • Geometric signature: symmetry group, lattice, branching, radiality, spiral, tessellation, fractal…  
  • Cross-domain analogy: where else does the analogous process appear?   (defaults to A)
  • Inferential use: what prediction about this phenomenon does the correspondence license, and has it been checked?   — e.g. the mass-spring ↔ circuit isomorphism predicts electrical ring-time from measured mechanical damping
Strength grades — strongest to weakest
  1. 1Shared mechanism — the same physical process operates at both scalesM C I
  2. 2Shared mathematics — the same equation or scaling law fits both; must be stated as an equation or ratioM C I
  3. 3Shared structure — the same geometry, demonstrablyM C I
  4. 4Shared function — the same role, different substrateI
  5. 5Aesthetic — visual resemblanceA
  6. 6Symbolic — meaning-level associationA

Label mapping: grades 1–4 may carry M/C/I when the shared mechanism or equation is cited; grades 5–6 are always A.

Guardrail · the category error of this lens

Shared form is not shared mechanism. A logarithmic spiral in a galaxy and in a nautilus shell arises from different processes, and resemblance transfers no properties between them. A correspondence claims exactly the relation it can state, at the grade it can defend.

Strong entry“The branching network minimizes transport cost at both leaf-vein and watershed scales — grade 2, shared optimization law” C
Weak entry“It spirals like a galaxy” — grade 5, labeled A or not kept.
SHEET IV

Rhythm — cycles and the pendulum

“Everything flows, out and in… the pendulum-swing manifests in everything.”Kybalion ch. XI
What cycles, pulses, flows, or recurrences occur — and along which polarity axes?

Boundary rule (Vibration vs. Rhythm). Sheet I records the frequency-domain identity — spectrum, modes, Q, the rates of motion. This sheet records the time-domain trajectory of state — period, phase, envelope, swings between poles. A rhythm is a slow vibration of state; placement is a bookkeeping choice, not a deep truth. Pick a sheet, state your cutoff (e.g. “period ≥ 1 s → Rhythm”), and cross-reference the other. The Kybalion's rhythm is the pendulum swing between poles — so this sheet consumes the Polarity table.

  • Primary cycle / period:  
  • Secondary cycles / modulation:  
  • Phase relative to environment (light, season, load, duty cycle):  
  • Growth / decay rates:  
  • Pattern: ☐ periodic  ☐ quasiperiodic  ☐ stochastic  ☐ event-driven  
  • Feedback: ☐ positive  ☐ negative  ☐ mixed  
  • Oscillating axes: for each axis in the Polarity table — does the state cycle along it? Record period, amplitude toward each pole, swing symmetry (the compensation check: are excursions toward Pole A and Pole B equal in size and duration?), and any drift of the neutral point.   (or mark the rhythm “axis-free”)
  • Temporal boundary conditions: which outside cycles drive it — light, temperature, power, human use?  

Negative feedback that damps the swing is the operational analog of the Kybalion's “neutralization” of rhythm A.

Guardrails · the category errors of this lens

Your checking schedule is not the thing's rhythm. Sample too slowly and you record fake cycles — aliasing, the wagon-wheel-spinning-backward effect. A single reading is a snapshot, not a rhythm.

Sample to the claim. Watch at least five full periods of the slowest cycle you intend to claim (five days for a daily cycle). Check faster than twice the quickest rhythm you care about — the Nyquist rule — comfortably five to ten times per beat, and use triggered capture for one-off events.

Strong entry“Day-night temperature swing ±1.5 °C along the thermal axis, symmetric, 5-day log” M
Weak entry“It has a daily energy cycle” — observed once, at one time of day.
SHEET V

Cause and Effect — the lawful chain

“Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause… chance is but a name for law not recognized.”Kybalion ch. XII
What produces it, changes it, or results from it?

Structure every causal claim as a chain, never a loose association:

Inputs / conditions   → Mechanism   → Observed state   → Effects  

  • Inputs: energy, material, field, stimulus, environment, user action  
  • Mechanism: a named physical, chemical, biological, or social process  
  • Outputs: measurable responses and consequences  
  • Feedback: how outputs alter later inputs  
  • Controls: which variables an experiment could vary  
  • Confounders: alternative explanations still open  
  • Causal confidence: established / likely / speculative / unknown — mapped onto the labels: established = mechanism backed by M/C; likely = I with cited evidence; speculative = A, or an I whose citation is still pending (cite or downgrade before marking the profile complete); unknown = U
Guardrail · the category error of this lens

“It happened after, so it was caused by” — the post hoc trap — and its cousin “they rise together, so one causes the other” are not mechanisms. Two synchronized cycles may share a hidden driver. Until a mechanism is named, the link stays an I with its confounders listed.

Strong entry“Squeeze → charge, via shifting of charged atom sites in a lopsided crystal grid; established” M
Weak entry“The readings changed after we moved it, so the new spot caused it” — succession, no mechanism, no confounders.
SHEET VI

Gender — complementary generative functions

“Gender is in everything; everything has its masculine and feminine principles.”Kybalion ch. XIII
What complementary generative roles couple here, and what does the coupling produce?

Admission test. A valid Gender entry names two roles different in kind whose coupling produces something neither produces alone. If the two terms are extremes of one measurable quantity, it belongs on Sheet II; if the pairing is exhausted by input → output, it belongs on Sheet V. This lens is a functional metaphor — never a biological or essentialist label.

  • Projective / emitting: what initiates, radiates, drives, differentiates, or imposes structure?  
  • Receptive / receiving: what accepts, contains, responds, integrates, or transforms input?  
  • Structure: what constrains and organizes the process?  
  • Flow: what moves, adapts, propagates, or develops within that structure?  
  • Product generated: what does the coupling produce that neither role contains alone?  

Worked micro-example. Piezoelectric coupling runs both ways: in the direct effect, applied squeeze is the initiating input and electrical polarization the response; in the converse effect, an applied voltage initiates and a flex responds. The projective/receptive roles belong to which port is being driven — a property of the interaction, not of the material — which is precisely why they suit a coupling-based lens. The conversion quality is scored by the coupling coefficient k. The role-reading is A; the physics is recorded on Sheet V M.

Guardrails · the category errors of this lens

Roles are not essences. The same quartz receives stress and emits charge, depending on which port is driven. “Quartz is a feminine stone” is this lens's category error; if kept at all, it is an A that generates no physical claims.

Reduction test. If an entry reduces without remainder to a Polarity axis or a Cause-and-Effect pair, record it there and drop it here.

Strong entry“Driven electrical port + elastic lattice → a flex neither produces alone, coupling coefficient k cited” M; role reading A
Weak entry“Quartz is a feminine stone” — an essence, no coupling, no product.
SHEET VII

Mentalism — how it is known

“THE ALL is Mind; the Universe is Mental.”Kybalion ch. IV–V
How is this phenomenon represented, perceived, modeled, or information-bearing?

Deliberately last: the claim labels used throughout this profile are this principle in action — every entry above is a representation, and this sheet audits how those representations were made.

  • Channels: which instruments make the phenomenon visible — camera, magnetometer, spectrometer, microphone, thermal sensor, chemical probe? What does each channel not see?  
  • Representation: what data model or category system stands between raw signal and profile entry?  
  • Information: what does the phenomenon encode, transmit, or constrain? Quantify as Shannon entropy (bits)   and signal-to-noise ratio (dB), stating what counts as signal, what counts as noise, and the bandwidth  . (Thermodynamic entropy is a different quantity — it belongs on Sheet II's order/disorder axis, not here.)
  • Direct vs. model-dependent: which entries above would change if the model changed?  
  • Measurement assumptions: calibration, bandwidth, resolution, sampling rate  
  • Representation feedback: does any model, expectation, setpoint, blueprint, or design intent of the phenomenon causally shape it? (Thermostat setpoints, design intent in artifacts, expectation effects in human systems.)  
  • The observers: what attention, expectation, or classification did we bring? (Observer effects in the everyday sense.)  

The source text asserts more — “THE ALL is Mind” — a metaphysical claim this profile neither needs nor makes; record it, if at all, as an A.

Guardrail · the category error of this lens

Instrument limits — noise floors, resolution, bandwidth — are facts about the measuring apparatus, not properties of the phenomenon; keep the apparatus's fingerprints separate from the object's features. And the observer effect, in either its everyday or its quantum sense, does not license “consciousness creates reality” claims.

Strong entry“Charge response visible only through the charge-amplifier channel; below 0.1 pC it drowns in amplifier noise, stated bandwidth 0.1–100 Hz” M (about the apparatus)
Weak entry“Observing it changes its energy” — unlabeled, and smuggling metaphysics through instrument language.

Worked example · the instrument in use

A quartz crystal, profiled

Header. Phenomenon: single ≈40 mm natural quartz point. Boundary: the crystal itself; mount and room are environment. Window: bench observation over five days. Instruments: calipers, polariscope, charge amplifier + multimeter, IR thermometer, temperature logger. Profile type: full (abridged here). Status: complete.

I · Vibration

Mechanical resonance modes are set by dimensions, cut, mounting, and loading C (from measured dimensions and literature stiffness values). This specimen's own mode spectrum: U → impedance or vibrometry sweep. For engineered cuts, tuning-fork resonators run 32.768 kHz with Q around 10⁴–10⁵ in vacuum, and AT-cut plates 1–30 MHz depending on thickness R (resonator literature). Visible appearance is the transmitted and scattered spectrum of room light — broadband, no selective absorption in this specimen M; quartz is birefringent, with refractive indices nₒ ≈ 1.544, nₑ ≈ 1.553 at 589 nm R (optical-mineralogy reference). “Reads as still, coherent” A.

II · Polarity

Under axial squeeze, piezoelectric charge separation opens a genuine electric axis with a zero point at rest M (charge amplifier); d₁₁ ≈ 2.3 pC/N R (resonator literature). A second, intrinsic axis: chirality — quartz grows left- or right-handed (enantiomorphic forms), the two distinguishable by optical rotation R (crystallography literature). This specimen's handedness: U → polarimetry. Thermal and mechanical-stress axes also measurable M. Primary axis: mechanical ↔ electric coupling I (from the charge-amplifier measurements).

III · Correspondence

Trigonal lattice symmetry (micro) expresses as prism-and-point habit and direction-dependent optics (meso) through crystal growth — grade 1–2, shared mechanism and shared symmetry mathematics I (cites the diffraction-established structure plus the growth model). Inferential use: the lattice symmetry predicts which faces develop and where the optic axis lies — checked against this specimen's habit and polariscope figure C. “Clarity of structure ↔ clarity of mind” — grade 6 A.

IV · Rhythm

In circuit use, a cut resonator sustains driven oscillation at resonance R (resonator literature). On the bench: day-night thermal cycling, swinging along the thermal polarity axis, amplitude ±1.5 °C, symmetric to within the logger's resolution — compensation check passes M (5-day temperature log); the neutral point shows a slow one-way drift over the log M, whose seasonal attribution is an inference I (the drift plus known seasonal forcing). Cutoff used: period ≥ 1 s → Rhythm.

V · Cause and Effect

Squeeze → polarization (direct effect), verified locally M (charge amplifier); the converse effect (voltage → flex) and its reversibility R (resonator literature); mechanism named — displacement of charged lattice sites in a non-centrosymmetric structure R (published crystal structure). The α–β transition at 573 °C changes the symmetry and with it the piezoelectric response R (mineralogy literature). Confounder logged: bench charge readings could include triboelectric artifacts; control: repeat with guarded electrodes I (cites the charge-amplifier measurement chain).

VI · Gender

Direct effect: squeeze (driven port) + lattice (structured medium) → charge separation; converse effect: voltage (driven port) → flex. Product generated: polarization, or strain, depending on which port is driven — the roles belong to the interaction, not the stone, with coupling strength k A as reading; the physics lives on Sheet V.

VII · Mentalism

Known through X-ray diffraction (lattice) R (published crystal structure), polariscopy (optic axis) M, and charge measurement (piezo response) M. Its frequency stability is why the world uses quartz to keep time — information-bearing in a precise, non-mystical sense I (cites resonator literature). The labels on this sheet are the audit trail. Traditional attributions (amplification, clarity) remain grade-6 symbolic entries A.

Measurement plan: mode spectrum of this specimen (impedance analyzer or laser vibrometry; easy); handedness (polarimeter; easy).
Label census: M/C-dominant; R entries name their sources; two U's, both in the plan; A entries present and firewalled. A physical portrait with an explicit, clearly bounded symbolic margin.
Synthesis. This quartz point is a trigonal SiO₂ single crystal I (identified from habit and optic figure) on a bench, profiled at hand scale over five days. Its vibrational identity is mechanically resonant C and optically transparent across the visible spectrum M; its primary polarity is stress-induced piezoelectric charge separation, zero at rest M, with chirality as a second intrinsic axis R; across scales its lattice symmetry repeats as facet geometry and optic axis, grade 1–2 I, with the habit and optic-axis predictions checked C; its bench rhythm is day-night thermal cycling along the thermal axis, symmetric M; squeeze causes charge M and voltage causes flex R, established and reversible; its generative coupling is driven port + structured lattice → charge or strain A. It is known through diffraction R, polariscopy M, and charge measurement M. Key unknowns are this specimen's mode spectrum and handedness U, resolvable by a vibrometry sweep and polarimetry. Taken whole: a measured physical portrait whose symbolic reputation for clarity rides alongside — labeled, and never mixed in A.
