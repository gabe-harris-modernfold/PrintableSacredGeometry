On the Eye as a Vortex

light, the effort of will, and the geometry of reception
The nature of infinity is this: That every thing has its / Own Vortex… William Blake · Milton, 1804–1810
…the sensations of the senses are tokens for our consciousness, it being left to our intelligence to learn how to comprehend their meaning. Hermann von Helmholtz · Handbuch der physiologischen Optik (Southall trans.)

In 1858 Hermann von Helmholtz published the paper that founded the mathematics of vortices. In the same years he was publishing the Handbuch der physiologischen Optik, the founding text of the science of the eye, and seven years earlier he had built the ophthalmoscope, the first instrument that let one person look into the living retina of another. No one else has stood in both rooms. This page puts his two subjects into one.

It is written by a mystic who keeps a ledger. Fire-eyed ancients, Blake's vortex, the evil eye and the third eye are all here, and so are receptive fields, logarithms and blind tests. Each claim wears its register, so you always know which kind of statement you are reading.

The proposition, as it was first put

Intelligibility begins with the observer's intent, which establishes the location of focus. The eye is understood as a receiving vortex whose sides constitute the assemblage of reality and whose tip is the point of focused apprehension. Through the vortex's structure and action, reality is filtered into comprehensible experience rather than independently assembled by the brain. Attention and optical focusing express a common pattern across scales: reception, gathering, convergence and resolution.

It left four questions open. This page answers each one:

  3. Is the correspondence across scales qualitative, or mathematically exact?
  4. Is a new force required to explain it?

I · The old quarrel

Which way does sight flow? Two thousand years of argument, and the shape that survived it

For two thousand years the West argued about whether something goes out of the eye when we see (extramission) or something comes in (intromission). Choose a thinker below. The arrow changes; the cone does not.

Extramission never died. Winer, Cottrell and colleagues (2002) found that a large share of American college students, around half on some ways of asking, say that something leaves the eyes when we see, and that physics courses barely change the answer. Measured

Then Guterstam, Graziano and colleagues (2019) found something subtler. People judging whether a paper tube will tip over are biased as if a nearby face's gaze were pushing it. Below belief, in the machinery itself, the brain models another person's gaze as a faint beam that carries force. Measured Graziano's reading is that this is how the brain sketches its own attention: as a substance flowing outward. The evil eye, the basilisk, Medusa and the Roman fascinatio would then be folk portraits of that sketch. Theory

The first lesson: the geometry survived every reversal of its arrow. Euclid's cone, Alhazen's cone and Kepler's cone are one cone. Optics confirms it exactly. By Helmholtz reciprocity, a light path traced from the eye to an object is the same path traced from the object to the eye. Exact Film-quality computer images are made this way: rays are cast from a virtual eye into the scene, and the picture comes out right because the geometry does not care which way the arrow points. The ancients were not wrong about the shape. They argued about the arrow.

II · The keystone

What goes out, and what comes in

The proposition calls the eye a receiving vortex, and it is offered as a framework for extramission. That looks like a contradiction. It is the centre of the whole thing, and the vortex is the only image I know that resolves it.

Watch a bathtub drain. Water flows inward and down; nothing flows out of the drain toward the rim. Yet the drain causes the whole pattern. When it opens, a drop in pressure spreads outward through the water at the speed of sound and sets every streamline in the basin moving. Exact The cause goes out; the substance comes in. The drain reaches across the whole basin without sending anything outward.

Fig. 2A draining vortex, opened
The pale front of influence travels outward first. The water inside it starts to spiral inward only once the front has passed; water beyond it has not yet been told. Nothing comes out of the drain. Speeds are not to scale: in water the front moves at about 1,500 m/s, the water at centimetres per second. Exact the streamlines are the logarithmic spirals of an ideal sink with swirl.

So the two ancient schools were each describing one half of a draining vortex:

  • The extramissionists described the direction of influence. Attention reaches out, selects and organizes, from the eye outward.
  • The intromissionists described the direction of energy. Light arrives, from the world inward.

In a vortex both are true at once, because the pressure gradient and the flow point in opposite directions.

Extramission of form, intromission of light. Intent spreads outward as an organizing gradient; light travels inward as substance. The eye is where the two directions coincide. Framework

Ezra Pound found the same double direction in 1914. He called the poetic image "a radiant node or cluster… a VORTEX, from which, and through which, and into which, ideas are constantly rushing." From which, through which, into which: both ways at once. Symbol

III · Where is the tip?

Two tips, an hourglass, and an eye that isn't there

The proposition hides an ambiguity it has to face. The tip is "the point of focus," "the location established by intent." But intent puts focus out there, on the cup, the word, the face. Optics puts focus in here, on the fovea. Which one is the tip?

Both. They are conjugate points: every optical focus has a twin on the other side of the lens. Exact Intent chooses the outer point, and then the body obeys. The eyes rotate, converge and reshape the lens (vergence and accommodation) until the inner twin lands on the fovea. The line joining the two is the visual axis: the vortex's axis. "If therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light" (Matthew 6:22). The single eye is that aligned axis, outer focus and inner focus on one line. Symbol

With two eyes, the figure becomes an hourglass. Each eye's optics converges inward to its own fovea; the two visual axes converge outward and cross at the point you are looking at. The optics of one eye puts the tip inside; the convergence of two eyes puts the tip outside. Drag the flame and the pencil below.

The tip is empty

  • Fovea means pit. At the very centre the inner retina is pushed aside so light falls on bare cones, and there are no capillaries at all. Measured
  • The very centre has no rods, so in starlight the exact point of gaze goes blind. Astronomers learn averted vision: to see a faint star, look beside it. Measured
  • Where all the retina's fibres gather and drain out of the eye, at the optic disc, there are no receptors. Mariotte found this blind spot in the 1660s, and the field closes over it so smoothly that most people never notice it in a lifetime. Measured
  • The eye of a hurricane is calm and clear.

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (3.4.2): you cannot see the seer of seeing. Wittgenstein (Tractatus 5.633–5.6331) drew the visual field as a teardrop with the eye at its point, and observed that nothing in the field shows that it is seen by an eye: "The subject does not belong to the world but it is a limit of the world." Dionysius spoke of a dazzling darkness; The Cloud of Unknowing teaches that the highest cannot be looked at straight on. Symbol

Anatomy and the apophatic tradition say the same thing: the tip is not a thing seen. It is the place from which nothing is seen, and around which everything is. Framework

IV · The sides

The assemblage, found

The proposition says the sides "constitute the assemblage of reality," that they are more than a boundary around a point. Vision science has found almost exactly this, though it never used the word vortex.

Peripheral vision does not record detail. It records summary statistics: the texture and gist of regions, pooled over zones that grow in proportion to their distance from the tip. Bouma's law (1970): neighbouring objects blur together when they are closer than about half their distance from the centre of gaze. Freeman and Simoncelli (2011) synthesized images scrambled throughout the periphery that look identical to the original while you fixate the centre, because each zone's statistics are preserved. And those zones are stretched along the lines that point toward the fovea (Toet & Levi, 1992), like streamlines aimed at the drain. Measured Try a home-made version.

Fix your eyes on the gold dot from normal reading distance and hold them there for a few seconds. Does the text around it look like ordinary text? Then look around. Away from the dot, every letter has been replaced at random by another of the same height class: the texture is kept and the words are gone. The sides cannot tell. Measured in principle (crowding); this demo is home-made.

The sides hold the world's gist; the tip holds its word. The rich world you feel around you is the sides' statistical assemblage, plus a tip that roams about three times a second, fetching detail wherever intent asks for it. Framework

The walls are aimed, too. The retina is built inside out, with the receptors at the back, and Müller glia carry light through the tissue in front of them like living optical fibres (Franze et al., 2007). The cones themselves are waveguides pointed at the centre of the pupil, which is why light entering through the pupil's centre counts for more than light near its edge (the Stiles–Crawford effect, 1933). If the pupil is displaced, they slowly re-aim. Measured

Embryology tells the same story. The retina is brain tissue. The optic vesicle first pushes outward from the forebrain toward the skin, and only afterwards folds inward into a cup. Measured The brain went out to meet the light before it cupped to receive it. In the body's own history, extramission comes first. Symbol Goethe said the eye owes its existence to light, which calls forth an organ like itself; Plotinus said no eye ever saw the sun without first becoming sunlike. Symbol

V · Scale invariance

Not a metaphor, a logarithm

The proposition asks whether the correspondence across scales is qualitative or mathematically exact. One exact correspondence exists, and it is beautiful.

1. The visual cortex is a logarithm. The map from the retina to primary visual cortex is, to a good approximation, the complex logarithm, w = log(z + a) (Schwartz, 1977). Measured The amount of cortex given to each degree of the field falls off as 1/(E + E₂), with E₂ ≈ 0.75° (Horton & Hoyt, 1991). Under a logarithm, enlarging about the fovea becomes a slide along one axis of the cortex and rotating becomes a slide along the other. Exact

2. The ideal vortex is the same logarithm. Flow into a swirling drain, a sink of strength Q joined to a vortex of circulation Γ, has the complex potential W(z) = −((Q + iΓ)/2π) log z. Its streamlines are logarithmic spirals whose pitch is set by the ratio of inflow to swirl, tan α = Q/Γ, and in log coordinates those spirals become straight parallel lines. Exact

Put the two together: a spiral vortex in the visual field is, on the cortex, exactly a uniform straight flow. This is not a resemblance. It is the same function. Pure inflow gives radial rays, pure swirl gives concentric rings, and any mixture gives spirals. On the cortex all three are simply stripes at different angles. Exact

The shimmering arc crawled outward across his visual field and sped up as it went, which is what a wave crossing the cortex at a steady ~3 mm per minute must look like through the logarithm. The waking brain is tuned to vortices as well. Neurons in area MST respond selectively to expanding, rotating and spiral motion (Graziano, Andersen & Snowden, 1994), the same Graziano who found the gaze-beam. Walk forward and the world streams outward from a single point, the focus of expansion, which marks where you are heading. The intent of moving sets the source point of the flow. Turn your gaze while walking and the flow becomes a spiral. People pick concentric and radial dot patterns out of noise from fewer aligned dots than parallel ones (Wilson & Wilkinson, 1998). VI · The presiding spirit

Helmholtz's three theorems, read through the eye

In 1858 Helmholtz proved three things about vortices in an ideal fluid, a fluid with no friction. Each has a counterpart in the eye: one exact, two that are analogies with consequences you can test, and a measured fact he would have enjoyed.

First theorem · vortex lines move with the fluid

In water. A vortex line is carried with the fluid like a thread dyed into it: the particles on a vortex line stay on it. Exact

In the eye. The axis of sight stays attached to the thing, not to the place. When a bird crosses the sky your eyes track it smoothly, and attention can ride several moving objects at once (Pylyshyn & Storm, 1988). Measured The tip belongs to the object, not to the coordinates. Framework

Second theorem · a vortex tube cannot end inside the fluid

In water. A vortex tube has the same strength along its whole length, so it can never simply stop in mid-fluid. It must close on itself, run to infinity, or end on a boundary: the tornado runs from cloud to ground, the smoke ring closes. Exact

In the eye. Give the eye a featureless field (empty sky, fog, a ganzfeld, the dark) and focus cannot rest at infinity. It falls back to a resting state about a metre away, the dark focus (Leibowitz & Owens, 1975); pilots scanning an empty sky are measurably short-sighted. Measured The axis of sight needs a surface at both ends. Framework

Third theorem · no vortex is born, none dies

In water. In an ideal fluid pushed only by conservative forces, the strength of a vortex never changes. No vortex can be created or destroyed without friction, a boundary, or an outside twist. Exact

In the eye. Light alone cannot make the vortex; something non-ideal must, a body that moves and a will that turns. And real vortices decay unless fed: an image held perfectly still on the retina fades in seconds. Measured Intent must keep turning the whirlpool. Framework

The bathtub test of the third theorem

In 1962 Ascher Shapiro at MIT filled a six-foot tank, let it settle for about a day so that no stirring remained, and pulled the plug. The drain turned counter-clockwise every time: the rotation of the Earth, far too faint to feel, gathered by the inflow until it showed. A team in Sydney later repeated it and got the opposite sense. Measured As water moves inward its spin speeds up, because the product of radius and swirl speed is conserved. Exact

So the drain does not create the swirl; it concentrates a swirl the field already holds. If the analogy holds, intent works the same way: it amplifies faint structure already present in the light rather than inventing structure from nothing, which is what attention's gain on neural signals does. Framework

The optical twin of the second theorem

Helmholtz has a second invariant, in optics. Through any lens system, the product of an image's height, the angle of its rays and the refractive index stays the same: the Lagrange–Helmholtz invariant. Exact It is the optical form of "the tube keeps its strength": squeeze the image, and its light must arrive more steeply. That is why the tip of the vortex cannot be both small and gentle.

Helmholtz's dissent

He would not have signed everything on this page. Helmholtz held that perception is unconscious inference: the brain reasons, without knowing it, from sensations to their most likely causes, and sensations are signs of the world, not pictures of it. Theory That stands against the proposition's claim that the brain does not assemble. On intent, though, he is the framework's ally. He argued that we judge where our eyes point from the effort of will it takes to move them, and the eye-press experiment in Practice 11 is his. VII · The force

The force, and where it has been measured

The conversation that began this page proposed an unknown, unmeasured force. To take it seriously, ask where such a force would have to show up. Part of it has been measured, though not as a push on matter.

When attention moves to a location, the receptive fields of neurons across the visual field shift toward it and shrink (Womelsdorf et al., 2006). That is literally streamlines bending toward a sink. Measured Click or tap anywhere in the field below to attend there.

Intent is part of what holds the world still

Close one eye and gently press the side of the other through the lower lid: the world swims. Move the same eye by choice and the world stays put. Helmholtz used this to argue that the brain counts its own intention to move when it keeps the world steady, through what is now called the efference copy. Measured In 1976 J. K. Stevens had himself partly paralysed with curare. When he tried to look sideways and the eye could not move, the world appeared to leap in the direction he intended (Stevens et al., 1976). Measured Intent alone, with no movement, displaced the world. There is no stronger evidence for the proposition's first sentence.

What has not survived measurement

  • E. B. Titchener addressed the feeling of being stared at in Science in 1898, and J. E. Coover tested it in 1913, without finding it. Measured
  • Rupert Sheldrake revived it in the 1990s with positive results. Contested Colwell, Schröder and Sladen (2000) found the effect disappeared once the stare sequences were truly randomized: subjects had been learning the pattern, not feeling the gaze. Measured
  • In Schlitz and Wiseman's first joint studies, a believer and a skeptic ran identical protocols and only the believer's sessions showed an effect; a larger later study found none for either. Contested
  • Baron Reichenbach's "sensitives" of the 1840s and 50s sat for hours in dark rooms and saw flames streaming from magnets and fingertips: dark-adapted eyes supplying whatever expectation asked for. Contested

The verdict: the framework does not need a new force, and it is stronger without one. The pattern recurs because a logarithmic draining vortex is the simplest way to gather a field onto a point under a scale-free law, and nature reuses its cheapest geometries. A new force would be needed only if a gaze could be detected with every light path blocked and the sequences truly random, or if a gaze could move a torsion balance. Those tests are clean, and the framework survives either result. Framework

VIII · Intent

Does intent create the vortex, redirect it, or locate it?

Alfred Yarbus (1967) recorded eyes looking at Repin's painting They Did Not Expect Him, in which a man in a worn coat walks in on a family. He gave the same viewer different questions: estimate the family's wealth, give their ages, say how long the man had been away. Each question produced a completely different path of eye movements. Measured The question moved the tip.

Simons and Chabris (1999) showed the other side of it. About half of viewers counting basketball passes missed a person in a gorilla suit who walked through the game and thumped their chest. Measured Intent places the tip, and it also narrows the mouth.

The fluid picture answers the open question. The water exists without the drain. Opening the drain creates the vortex, and where you open it is a choice. Intent does not make the field of light; it makes the whirlpool, and it places the tip. Shapiro's tank adds one refinement: the whirlpool's spin is gathered from the field, not added to it. Framework

The whirlpool has to keep turning. An image held perfectly still on the retina, by optical tracking or Yarbus's suction caps, fades to grey within seconds (Ditchburn; Riggs; 1950s). Hold your gaze steady and the edges of the scene dissolve (Troxler fading, 1804). Fill the whole field with uniform featureless light and sight blanks out. Measured The eye never stops trembling, with microsaccades and drift, because a still vortex is no vortex. Focus is not a point; it is a balance held by motion.

Norbert Wiener: "We are but whirlpools in a river of ever-flowing water." Poe's fisherman in "A Descent into the Maelström" survived by doing what the others did not: he watched. He saw that cylinders sank slowest, and lashed himself to a cask. Calm attention held inside the vortex revealed its law. Symbol

IX · Receiver or assembler

The brain: receiver, or assembler?

This is the proposition's boldest claim, so it deserves both its allies and its opponents.

Allies

  • Bergson (Matter and Memory, 1896): perception is subtraction, a selection from everything that exists, measured by what the body could do; the brain is "a kind of central telephonic exchange" that connects rather than creates. Theory
  • William James (Human Immortality, 1898) separated the brain's productive function from its transmissive one. Theory
  • Blake: "If the doors of perception were cleansed every thing would appear to man as it is, Infinite. For man has closed himself up, till he sees all things thro' narrow chinks of his cavern." Huxley took his "reducing valve" from C. D. Broad and his title from Blake. Symbol
  • J. J. Gibson (1979): vision picks up patterns already present in the light around us, with no reconstruction. Theory
  • The most respectable ally: J. Kevin O'Regan's "world as an outside memory" (1992), and the change-blindness experiments (Rensink, O'Regan & Clark, 1997): large changes to a scene go unseen across a brief flicker. The brain does not keep a detailed inner picture; the detail stays in the world, fetched by the tip on demand. Measured

Opponents

  • Helmholtz's unconscious inference and its modern heir, predictive processing. Theory
  • The brain fills in the blind spot with pattern and colour (Practice 7 shows it). Measured
  • Above all, dreams: whole worlds with the eyes closed. Measured

Looked at closely, though, the dream becomes the framework's best witness. Lucid dreamers test whether they are dreaming by reading a line of text, looking away and reading it again, because in dreams the text rarely survives the second reading. Clocks will not keep their time. Measured The brain can spin the vortex with no water in it: the funnel shapes and the motion are all there. What it cannot do is make the content hold still, because there is no outside memory to return to.

Stable content is the signature of reception. Framework

These practices are offered for education and contemplation. They are not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment, they are not a vision test, and they are no substitute for an examination by an eye doctor. Nothing here has been reviewed by a clinician for your circumstances.

If you have an eye or neurological condition (glaucoma, retinal disease, recent eye surgery, high myopia, epilepsy or photosensitivity, migraine with aura) or a history of dissociation, panic or psychosis, ask your eye doctor or physician before trying them. Stop at once if you feel pain, strain, dizziness, nausea or distress.

New flashes of light, a sudden shower of floaters, or a shadow or curtain across your vision can mean a retinal tear or detachment and need medical care the same day. You undertake these practices at your own risk.

These practices have a patron saint: Jan Evangelista Purkinje. In Prague in the 1810s and 1820s he experimented on himself. He waved his spread fingers before his closed eyes while facing the sun, took drugs to watch his vision change, and recorded everything his own eyes did. He found the tree of vessels inside the eye, the colour shift at dusk and the flicker figures, and Goethe championed him. Goethe also gave this work its rule: "Do not look for anything behind the phenomena; they themselves are the teaching."

II · The walls

Seeing the structure

4The tree in the eyebright wall or sky · a card

DoMake a hole in a piece of dark card with a needle. Hold the pinhole right in front of one eye, look through it at a bright white wall or an overcast sky, and move the card in small, quick circles. What appearsA branching tree of dark vessels fills your vision, all its branches converging on one root off to the side, and exactly where you are looking there is a small clearing with no vessels in it.

WhyYour retinal blood vessels lie in front of your light receptors and cast shadows on them. The shadows never move relative to the retina, so the visual system erases them: images held still on the retina always fade. Moving the light moves the shadows, and they appear. ReadingYou have lived your whole life looking through a tree you have never seen. This is the first law of the walls: whatever moves with the seer is erased. Keep hold of it; it returns at the end in its strangest form. Framework

9The tip that goes darka dark night · dusk

    The consolidated statement, refined

    Intelligibility begins with the observer's intent. Intent does not create the field of light; it opens the vortex within it, gathering a swirl the field already holds, and establishes where the focus lies.

    The vortex has two linked tips: an outer focus chosen in the world and an inner focus on the fovea, joined by the axis of sight. With two eyes it becomes an hourglass, felt from a virtual centre between them. The axis must end on surfaces at both ends.

    Influence travels outward as an organizing gradient: attention reaching, receptive fields bending toward the focus, perceived space compressing onto it. Light travels inward as substance. Extramission and intromission are the two directions of one draining vortex.

    The sides make up the assemblage: a statistical gathering of the field, pooled in zones that grow with distance from the tip and point toward it. The tip resolves the particular, and is itself empty: the pit, the drain, the calm eye of the storm.

    Across scales the correspondence is exact wherever the pattern is self-similar. The cortical map and the ideal spiral vortex are the same logarithm, and when the visual brain runs without a world it shows the vortex's three streamline families as Klüver's form constants. The correspondence breaks at the tip, where each medium's own grain appears.

    Experience requires field, structure and intent together, a Borromean knot. Without the field, content will not hold still; without intent, nothing is seen; without the structure, the hand finds what the mind cannot see. No new force is required, but the framework names exactly where one would have to show itself, and how to test for it blind.

    Influence goes out; light comes in. The sides are universal; the tip is local. Intent does not make the water; it makes the whirlpool.

    Goethe gave the reason any of this is worth doing: "Every object, well contemplated, opens a new organ in us." That is the framework turned back on the one who practises it. Intent does not only shape what the vortex receives; over time it shapes the receiver.
