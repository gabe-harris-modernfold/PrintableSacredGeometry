From One Circle

a matched set of sacred geometry to print, every piece grown from one 15 mm circle
“God is always doing geometry.” — PLATO, AS REPORTED BY PLUTARCH, TABLE TALK VIII.2

Sacred-geometry prints usually come from different places at different sizes, so a Flower of Life never quite matches the Platonic solids it is supposed to contain. This set fixes that. Every piece starts from a single circle with a radius of 15 mm, the one measure. Eight flat plates take you from that circle to Metatron’s Cube and the Tree of Life, and they stack on pins with every line in register. From the plates come the five Platonic solids, their stars, and two clusters of spheres, and each one sits on its plate at exactly the right size.

Go step by step, or jump to printing the set. Every step has a drawing, a 3D view you can turn, and the file to print. Two labels keep the mathematics and the meaning apart: exact marks geometry you can check with a ruler, and tradition marks names, history and symbolism.

00The one measure exact

Three rules size every piece. Each one is a single sentence, and each can be checked on the prints with a ruler.

Flat figures

Every circle has radius R = 15 mm, and every centre sits on one triangular grid of points 15 mm apart, one row running straight up. All eight plates share a 170 mm ring and three pins, so their lines fall on one another when stacked.

Solids

Look straight down a solid’s three-fold axis, through the middle of a triangular face or a three-way corner. Its outermost corners then land on the circle of radius 4R = 60 mm that runs through the six outer centres of Metatron’s Cube.

Spheres

Spheres of radius R sit on a cube-shaped grid with a spacing of R × √(3/2) = 18.37 mm. Seen corner-on, that grid’s shadow is the plates’ grid, so a 2 × 2 × 2 cluster casts the Seed of Life and a 3 × 3 × 3 cluster casts the Flower of Life.

The grid is triangular, not square, because six equal circles fit exactly around a seventh: six equilateral triangles meet at a point, and 6 × 60° = 360°. That one fact is why every figure below closes up with nothing left over.

01The Circle

exact

One circle, radius 15 mm. Every later figure is made the same way: put the compass point on a place where lines already cross, and draw another circle of the same size.

tradition

The circle stands for unity and wholeness in many traditions: no beginning, no end, every point the same distance from the centre.

02Vesica Piscis

exact

Each circle passes through the other’s centre. The lens between them is 15 mm wide and 15 × √3 = 26.0 mm tall, a ratio of exactly √3. Join the two centres and either crossing point and you get a perfect equilateral triangle, which is how Euclid begins his Elements (Book I, Proposition 1).

tradition

Vesica piscis is Latin for “fish’s bladder”. In medieval Christian art the lens becomes the mandorla, the almond-shaped glory around Christ in Majesty.

03Tripod of Life

exact

Three circles, each through both other centres. The centres form an equilateral triangle with 15 mm sides, the first triangle of the grid.

tradition

“Tripod of Life” is a modern name from sacred-geometry teaching. The pattern also echoes the Borromean rings and the Christian triquetra.

04Seed of Life, and the Egg of Life

exact

Keep drawing around the first circle and six circles fit exactly, each through the centre and through its two neighbours’ centres. Their centres make a regular hexagon with 15 mm sides.

The Egg of Life is the same figure in three dimensions: eight spheres of radius 15 mm at the corners of a cube with 18.37 mm sides. Look at it corner-on and two spheres hide behind each other in the middle while the other six ring them, casting this plate exactly. The spheres overlap, so it prints as one solid, 48 mm across.

tradition

The Seed of Life is often read as the seven days of creation in Genesis, one circle per day. “Seed”, “Egg” and “Fruit of Life” are modern names, popularised in the 1990s.

05Flower of Life, in circles and in spheres

exact

Go two rings out from the centre and you have 19 circles, every one on the grid. The petal shapes are vesicas, all the same size as the one on plate 02. The border circle has radius 3R = 45 mm and just touches the six outermost circles.

In spheres: 27 spheres in a 3 × 3 × 3 block, on the same cube grid as the Egg. Seen corner-on, the 27 cast exactly these 19 circles, because some sit behind others. It is 67 mm across.

tradition

Drawings of the pattern on granite columns of the Osireion at Abydos, Egypt, are often cited as the oldest. They are drawn, not carved, and their date is disputed. The name “Flower of Life” and most of its present-day meaning come from late-20th-century writing, especially Drunvalo Melchizedek’s.

06Fruit of Life

exact

Thirteen circles that only touch, with centres 30 mm (2R) apart along the grid’s six directions, plus six more at 60 mm (4R). Every centre is still a point of the grid: along each of the six directions, the Fruit keeps every second circle of an extended Flower. Its outer circles end at 5R = 75 mm, which is why the ring frame starts there.

tradition

Thirteen is read as the number of the “Fruit”, from which Metatron’s Cube is drawn.

07Metatron’s Cube

exact

Join every one of the Fruit’s 13 centres to every other: 13 × 12 ÷ 2 = 78 lines. Hexagons, six-pointed stars and the outline of a cube seen corner-on all appear. The next step tests the claim that all five Platonic solids are hidden here.

tradition

Named after the archangel Metatron of Jewish mystical writing. The figure as drawn here is modern.

08Five solids in one drawing

A Platonic solid has identical regular faces and the same number of faces meeting at every corner. There are exactly five, which Euclid proves at the end of the Elements (Book XIII). Here each is printed as an edge frame: 5 mm struts with 7 mm nodes at the corners. Pick one and see where its corners fall on Metatron’s Cube.

Tetrahedron

tetrahedron_frame.stl
Why the last two miss: the dodecahedron and icosahedron are built on the golden ratio, φ = 1.618…, which no whole-number grid can hold. Metatron’s Cube has rings only at 30 and 60 mm, in the plain ratio 1 : 2, so their remaining corners fall between the rings. The Forbidden Symmetry page follows the same φ into crystals, where it makes five-fold symmetry impossible.

09Stars, and the woven five

Stellation means extending a solid’s faces outward until they meet again. Each face then grows a pyramid whose sides lie in the planes of its neighbouring faces. Each star below is grown from the solid in step 08, so its core is exactly that solid. The tetrahedron and cube cannot be stellated, because their faces meet at 90° or less and the extended planes never close in front of a face. For those two, the set uses the classic stand-in instead: the solid combined with its partner solid, called its dual, which swaps corners for face centres.

The five intersecting tetrahedra

exact

The dodecahedron’s 20 corners split into five groups of four, and each group is a regular tetrahedron. Each tetrahedron’s edge is the cube’s face diagonal, 103.9 mm. Built as flat-faced frames, the five pass through one another without touching.

The beam width is calculated, not guessed. The widest beam that still lets the frames slip past each other is the edge length ÷ 12.09. That matches Francis Ow’s origami unit folded from a 1 × 3 strip, whose width is the edge ÷ 12. The woven file prints the five frames already interlocked, with 0.8 mm between them. The rigid file welds them into one solid.

10Tree of Life

exact

Every sephirah sits on a circle centre of the Flower of Life (plate 05), with Tiferet in the middle. The Tree is 60 mm from Kether at the top to Malkuth at the bottom, exactly four grid steps. Stack this plate on the Flower and every disc lands on a circle centre.

tradition

The Tree of Life is the central diagram of Kabbalah, the Jewish mystical tradition. This layout of ten sephirot and 22 paths is the one Athanasius Kircher published in 1652, later adopted by the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn. Each path is paired with a letter of the Hebrew alphabet.

11Printing the set

All files are for a 320 mm bed in PETG with a 0.4 mm nozzle, and every one was checked to be a single, watertight piece that fits the bed. The edge frames come already seated on their widest face, with a thin flat underneath to help them stick.

Stacking the plates

Print plate_pin.stl three times; it lies on its flat side so it needs no supports. Or use 3 mm dowels. The pin holes are 3.4 mm, at 0°, 120° and 180° around the ring. Because they are spaced unevenly, a plate only seats one way round and can’t go on rotated or flipped. Stack any plates in any order and their lines fall on one another. Circle, Seed, Flower, Tree makes a good four-layer stack.

12Exact, and tradition

exact These are checked numerically by the scripts that make the files, and you can confirm them on the prints:

  • All eight plates share one grid. Stacked on the pins, their circle centres coincide.
  • Corner-on, the tetrahedron, cube and octahedron put every corner on a centre of Metatron’s Cube.
  • The dodecahedron puts 8 of its 20 corners there (the cube hidden inside it), and the icosahedron 6 of 12. The rest cannot fit, because of φ.
  • At these sizes the cube and octahedron are exact partners: their edges cross at their midpoints.
  • The Egg of Life and the 27-sphere Flower cast exactly the Seed and Flower plates.
  • Every sephirah of the Tree lies on a circle centre of the Flower of Life.

tradition The names Seed, Egg and Fruit of Life, the idea that the Flower of Life is the pattern of creation, Metatron as the figure’s angel, and the Kabbalistic meanings of the Tree are traditions of interpretation, some ancient and some quite recent. This page reports them; the geometry neither proves nor disproves them.

correction The popular claim that Metatron’s Cube “contains all five Platonic solids” holds exactly for three and only approximately for the other two (step 08).
