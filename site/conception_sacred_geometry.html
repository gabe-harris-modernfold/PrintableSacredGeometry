Sources & what is being claimed

This page is a work of interpretation. It is built so that you can always tell which register a statement belongs to, because the three make very different kinds of claim.

Observed — established, and checkable

  • The human secondary oocyte is roughly 120 µm across — the largest cell in the body — and sits arrested in metaphase II until fertilisation. The sperm head is about 4–5 µm. The size ratio shown in movement II is real, not dramatised.
  • The calcium wave. Sperm delivers phospholipase C zeta (PLCζ), which makes IP₃, which opens IP₃ receptors — a Ca²⁺ wave beginning at the fusion site and crossing the egg, followed by hours of oscillations.
  • The zinc spark is real and published — Que et al., Nature Chemistry 2015 (mouse); Duncan et al., Scientific Reports 2016 (human eggs). Two honest caveats: it is a fluorescent-probe signal, not emitted light — the “flash at conception” headlines were press invention — and the human work used chemically activated eggs.
  • The two pronuclei are real and are the anatomical home of the vesica on this page. After sperm entry the maternal and paternal pronuclei form as two separate, roughly equal spheres, migrate together, and appose at the centre. IVF laboratories grade this 2PN stage daily.
  • Syngamy is not a fusion of two nuclei into one nucleus. The pronuclear envelopes break down and the two chromosome sets assemble on a single first mitotic spindle. There is no moment where one round nucleus contains both — the merged sphere in movement V is the ideal register, not the observed one.
  • Endogenous currents and fields are real. Steady transcellular ionic currents were measured in eggs and embryos decades ago by Jaffe and Nuccitelli with vibrating probes, and electrotaxis is well established. Most recently Ferreira, Moreira, Zhao & Barriga, Nature Materials 2025, showed endogenous electric fields steering neural crest in Xenopus via voltage-sensitive phosphatase 1.

Ideal — true as geometry, idealised as biology

  • The vesica piscis construction is exact. Two circles of radius R whose centres are R apart intersect in a lens of height R√3 and width R — a ratio of √3 : 1. Its corners and centres give the equilateral triangle, and from that triangle come the hexagon, the Seed of Life and the Flower of Life. That much is provable.
  • What is idealised is that real pronuclei are not perfect equal spheres and are not reliably placed at exactly one radius apart. The √3 is put there by the construction, not measured off an embryo.
  • The Egg of Life is drawn as eight equal spheres on the vertices of a cube — that is, as two interpenetrating tetrahedra. Real 8-cell human embryos compact: cells flatten, polarise and maximise contact, and human cleavage is famously irregular and asynchronous. The regular solid is the ideal, never the observation.
  • A closed, axially symmetric current circuit necessarily has toroidal topology — in at one pole, through the interior, out at the other, back around outside. That is honest mathematics. What is not established is a stable toroidal bioelectric envelope around the human zygote. Note also that the 2025 result above runs the other way round: there the field is generated by tissue mechanics, downstream of stretch, not an organising field the tissue obeys.

Hermetic — attributed carefully, because the dates matter

  • Emerald Tablet (Tabula Smaragdina). Earliest known text is Arabic, c. 8th–9th century, in the Kitāb Sirr al-Khalīqa; Latin translations from the 12th century. Attributed to Hermes Trismegistus — not an ancient Egyptian document, despite the legend.
  • Corpus Hermeticum. Greek philosophical dialogues, 2nd–3rd century CE, Roman Egypt. Renaissance readers believed them far older; Isaac Casaubon showed in 1614 that they are not.
  • Liber XXIV philosophorum, 12th-century Latin, pseudo-Hermetic. Source of “a sphere whose centre is everywhere and circumference nowhere.”
  • The Kybalion, 1908, published as by “Three Initiates” and generally attributed to William Walker Atkinson. This is the source of the familiar seven principles — Mentalism, Correspondence, Vibration, Polarity, Rhythm, Cause and Effect, Gender — used as the spine of these seven movements. It is American New Thought from 1908, not ancient Hermetica. Naming it plainly costs nothing and keeps the rest of the page trustworthy.
  • The correspondences drawn here between these texts and embryology are mine, offered as interpretation. No Hermetic author knew about pronuclei, calcium waves or zinc.

The claim, stated plainly

Not that the ancients encoded embryology. Rather: the vesica piscis is what you get whenever two equal spheres meet centre-to-centre, and at exactly one moment in human development two roughly equal spheres do meet that way, carrying one genome each. The tradition put its founding figure at the place where two become one. Conception puts a real instance of that figure in the same place. Whether that is resonance or coincidence is not something geometry can settle — but the figure is genuinely there, and it is worth looking at.
