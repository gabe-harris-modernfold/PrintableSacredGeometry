(`the-transformer.png`). If your generator accepts a style reference, attach that image with each prompt.

Every prompt ends with the same style paragraph so the set stays consistent. The prompts forbid lettering,
because generators garble text; the page supplies the captions. Save each result in this folder under
the filename given, and it can be optimised to WebP and placed.

## 1 · The two roads to motion (3:2)

An alchemical emblem of two roads that turn the same wheel. A small radiant sun at top centre shines down on both halves of the picture. On the left, a sheaf of wheat and a loaf of bread; a bare-armed labourer who has eaten the bread hauls directly on a rope that turns a wooden wheel. There is no fire anywhere on the left road, only a faint warm breath leaving the labourer's mouth. On the right, a lump of coal bearing the imprint of a fossil fern feeds a furnace; the furnace heats a copper boiler, its steam drives a piston, and the piston turns an identical wooden wheel. Curling flames and smoke escape from the furnace chimney and the boiler. The two wheels are the same size and face each other across the centre line. The left road is short and direct; the right road is a longer chain that passes through fire.

Style: antique alchemical emblem engraving, fine black-ink line work with cross-hatching and stippling, on aged cream parchment with foxing, faint stains and worn edges, framed by a double-ruled border with small corner flourishes. Sepia and black ink with sparing accents of vermilion red and burnished gold. Clear, uncluttered composition with generous margins. No text, letters, numbers, labels, captions or watermark.

*Getting it right:* the muscle road must have no furnace. It still gives off some heat (the warm breath), because the difference between the roads is a single step versus a chain, not loss versus no loss.

## 2 · The tithe of heat (3:2)

An alchemical emblem of an inheritance that dwindles as it passes down. A blazing sun in the upper left pours a broad golden stream onto a staircase of seven emblematic stations descending from left to right: a great tree, a black lump of coal, a furnace with a boiler, a turbine wheel turned by steam, a glass jar holding a captive lightning bolt, a small lantern, and finally a single human eye. Each station hands the golden stream down to the next, and the stream grows visibly thinner at every step. At each station part of the stream breaks away and falls as flame into one long stone fire-trough that runs along the bottom of the picture: the common grave of heat. The largest spill falls from the turbine, a large one from the lantern, and only a thin trickle from the lightning jar. Even the faint ray that passes from the lantern to the eye ends by sinking into the same trough.

Style: antique alchemical emblem engraving, fine black-ink line work with cross-hatching and stippling, on aged cream parchment with foxing, faint stains and worn edges, framed by a double-ruled border with small corner flourishes. Sepia and black ink with sparing accents of vermilion red and burnished gold. Clear, uncluttered composition with generous margins. No text, letters, numbers, labels, captions or watermark.

*Getting it right:* the spill sizes follow the page's coal chain. The heat engine loses the most (more than half), the lamp loses a lot, and the generator and wires lose little.

## 3 · The valley of iron (3:2)

An alchemical emblem of a valley where every element comes to rest. A deep valley is seen in profile across the full width. The left wall is a towering, almost vertical cliff; at its summit sits a small glowing sphere, and partway down a narrow ledge holds a slightly larger sphere. The valley floor, a little right of centre, holds a heavy black iron anvil on solid rock: the lowest, most settled point. The right wall rises only gently and to a modest height, about one-eighth of the left cliff, and a great boulder rests at its top. Two transformations roll downhill toward the anvil, both shedding light as they go. On the left, two small spheres merge into one and tumble down the steep cliff trailing golden sun-rays. On the right, the great boulder splits into two middle-sized stones that roll down the gentle slope trailing sparks. Above the left cliff a star burns; its flames reach down the cliff but stop at the anvil.

Style: antique alchemical emblem engraving, fine black-ink line work with cross-hatching and stippling, on aged cream parchment with foxing, faint stains and worn edges, framed by a double-ruled border with small corner flourishes. Sepia and black ink with sparing accents of vermilion red and burnished gold. Clear, uncluttered composition with generous margins. No text, letters, numbers, labels, captions or watermark.

*Getting it right:* this draws the binding-energy curve upside down, as an energy valley, so that both fusion and fission plainly *fall* toward iron and release energy. The page's "iron peak" is the same fact seen from the other side.
- The shape matters: a very steep, tall left wall with a ledge for helium-4, which is unusually tightly bound.
- The right wall is long, low and gentle, with uranium only slightly above the floor compared with hydrogen's height.

## 4 · The near miss (2:3)

An alchemical emblem of lead becoming gold for an instant. A long straight tunnel of riveted iron rings recedes vertically down through the picture. Near the top, two nuclei race past each other in opposite directions. Each is a cluster of many small, tightly packed spheres, flattened by its speed into a thin disc, and both are dull lead-grey. They never touch: a clear gap separates them, and fine arcing lines of force reach across the gap from one disc to the other. From the struck disc exactly three small spheres burst out and fly away. Further down the tunnel the same disc now glows burnished gold, and further still it shatters into fragments against the tunnel wall. In the top-left corner sits an old Saturn figure with a scythe, the emblem of lead; in the top-right corner, a radiant sun with a face, the emblem of gold.

Style: antique alchemical emblem engraving, fine black-ink line work with cross-hatching and stippling, on aged cream parchment with foxing, faint stains and worn edges, framed by a double-ruled border with small corner flourishes. Sepia and black ink with sparing accents of vermilion red and burnished gold. Clear, uncluttered composition with generous margins. No text, letters, numbers, labels, captions or watermark.

*Getting it right:* the nuclei must not collide. The flattening is real (relativistic contraction), the field acts across the gap, three protons leave (82 → 79), and the gold lasts only an instant.

## 5 · One heat, two readings (2:3)

An alchemical emblem of one body read two ways. A cross-legged meditating figure sits on a snowy mountain ledge, eyes closed, belly drawn in on a held breath; the snow has melted in a neat circle around them. A fine vertical line divides the figure exactly down the middle. The left half is drawn as an anatomical engraving: lungs, ribs and diaphragm, the muscles of the belly, the blood vessels of the hand and foot. A slender glass thermometer with a red thread touches the fingertips, another rests at the toes, and a pocket watch hangs nearby. The right half is drawn as a Daoist inner-alchemy landscape: a small furnace glowing below the navel, a channel rising up the spine like a ladder of light, and a cauldron in the head from which a thin vapour rises. Behind the left side, a few brass scientific instruments stand on a shelf; behind the right side, a coiled salamander rests in flames. The two halves meet seamlessly along the dividing line.

Style: antique alchemical emblem engraving, fine black-ink line work with cross-hatching and stippling, on aged cream parchment with foxing, faint stains and worn edges, framed by a double-ruled border with small corner flourishes. Sepia and black ink with sparing accents of vermilion red and burnished gold. Clear, uncluttered composition with generous margins. No text, letters, numbers, labels, captions or watermark.

*Getting it right:* the two halves are kept separate on purpose. The instruments read a warming body (breath and muscle, as Benson and Kozhevnikov measured); the right half is the tradition's reading of the same heat. The picture should not show one half proving the other.
