# Bifilar Rodin Coil Digital Twin — Comprehensive Mathematics Study

## Executive Summary

This study provides the complete mathematical foundation for building a digital twin of a bifilar Rodin coil, covering: toroidal geometry and parametric winding coordinates; the VBM-derived winding skip pattern; bifilar counter-winding topology; magnetic field modeling via Biot-Savart and Ampère's Law; and the frequency-, PWM-, voltage-, and wattage-dependent dynamics needed for a live-adjustable simulation.

---

## Part I — Toroidal Geometry

### Torus Coordinate System

A torus is parameterized in three dimensions by two angles: the **toroidal angle** \(\phi\) (revolution around the main axis) and the **poloidal angle** \(\theta\) (revolution around the tube cross-section). Given major radius \(R\) (center of hole to tube center) and minor radius \(a\) (tube radius):

\[
\begin{aligned}
x(\phi, \theta) &= (R + a\cos\theta)\cos\phi \\
y(\phi, \theta) &= (R + a\cos\theta)\sin\phi \\
z(\phi, \theta) &= a\sin\theta
\end{aligned}
\quad \phi, \theta \in [0, 2\pi)
\]

The **aspect ratio** is \(A = R/a\). For the Rodin coil described in published measurements, a representative geometry is ([Vortex Magnetic Induction Mathematical Geometric A](https://www.scribd.com/document/788613846/Vortex-Magnetic-Induction-Mathematical-Geometric-A)):

| Parameter | Symbol | Example Value |
|---|---|---|
| Outer diameter | \(D\) | 9.95 cm |
| Aperture diameter | \(2R_c\) | 2.9 cm |
| Major radius | \(R\) | \((D - 2R_c)/2 + R_c\) |
| Minor radius | \(a\) | \(R - R_c\) |
| Number of poles | \(N_{poles}\) | 12 (or 36) |

## Part II — Rodin Winding Pattern Mathematics

### VBM Doubling Circuit

The Rodin winding geometry derives from the **Vortex-Based Mathematics (VBM)** doubling sequence ([Rex Research — Basis for the Extraordinary Rodin Coil](http://www.rexresearch.com/rodin/2-vbm.pdf)):

\[
d = \{2, 4, 8, 7, 5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 7, 5, 1, \ldots\} \quad \text{(period 6, reduces powers of 2 mod 9)}
\]

This is generated by decimal parity (repeated digit-sum) reduction of successive doublings of 2:

\[
a_{n+1} = \text{dep}(2 \cdot a_n), \quad a_1 = 2
\]

The complementary reverse-doubling series is:

\[
b = \{1, 5, 7, 8, 4, 2, \ldots\} \quad b_j = d_{7 - (j \bmod 6)} \text{ for } j \bmod 6 \ne 0
\]

The **3-6-9 axis** forms a separate oscillating vector:

\[
e = \{6, 6, 9, 3, 3, 9, 6, 6, 9, \ldots\}
\]

These three series tile the 2D torus surface matrix \(t_{jk}\) ([Blake — Towards a Mathematical Formulation of the Rodin Coil Torus](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/237fed4a-3b57-45d0-8ae7-90a22ec3a972/6-blakemathformulation.pdf)):

\[
t_{jk} = \begin{cases}
m_{8,\,\text{dep}(\text{dep}(2j+1)+3(k-1))} & j \text{ odd}, k \text{ odd} \\
m_{1,\,\text{dep}(\text{dep}(2(j+2))+3(k-2))} & j \text{ odd}, k \text{ even} \\
m_{1,\,\text{dep}(\text{dep}(2j+1)+3(k-1))} & j \text{ even}, k \text{ odd} \\
m_{8,\,\text{dep}(\text{dep}(2(j+2))+3(k-2))} & j \text{ even}, k \text{ even}
\end{cases}
\]

where \(m_i\) denotes the decimal-parity multiplication series for digit \(i\), and repetition is \(t_{jk} = t_{(j \bmod 18)(k \bmod 18)}\).

The standard 12-pole Rodin coil places poles at angular intervals of \(30°\). The winding **skips 5 poles** (150°) each step, generating the sequence ([YouTube — How to Wind a Rodin Coil](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WiwXW5fgO4o)):

\[
\text{Pole sequence: } 1 \to 6 \to 11 \to 4 \to 9 \to 2 \to 7 \to 12 \to 5 \to 10 \to 3 \to 8 \to 1
\]

In general, for \(N_p\) poles and angular skip \(\alpha_s\):

\[
\text{pole}_{n+1} = \left(\text{pole}_n + \frac{\alpha_s}{360°} \cdot N_p\right) \bmod N_p
\]

For 12 poles and 150°: skip = \(150/30 = 5\) poles.

For 36 poles and 150°: skip = \(150/10 = 15\) poles ([Vortex Magnetic Induction Mathematical Geometric A](https://www.scribd.com/document/788613846/Vortex-Magnetic-Induction-Mathematical-Geometric-A)).

### Wire Path Parametric Equations

Each conductor travels along the torus surface with a specific poloidal-to-toroidal winding ratio. For a wire completing \(N_w\) wraps for one full toroidal circuit, the parametric path is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi(t) &= t \\
\theta(t) &= \frac{N_w \cdot \alpha_s}{2\pi} \cdot t + \phi_0
\end{aligned}
\quad t \in [0, 2\pi \cdot N_{turns}]
\]

In 3D Cartesian:

\[
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= (R + a\cos\theta(t))\cos\phi(t) \\
y(t) &= (R + a\cos\theta(t))\sin\phi(t) \\
z(t) &= a\sin\theta(t)
\end{aligned}
\]

The differential arc element for Biot-Savart integration is:

\[
d\vec{l} = \left(\frac{dx}{dt}, \frac{dy}{dt}, \frac{dz}{dt}\right) dt
\]

with components ([Vortex Magnetic Induction Mathematical Geometric A](https://www.scribd.com/document/788613846/Vortex-Magnetic-Induction-Mathematical-Geometric-A)):

\[
d\vec{l} = dr\,\hat{b}_r + r\,d\theta\,\hat{b}_\theta + r\sin\theta\,d\phi\,\hat{b}_\phi
\]

## Part III — Bifilar Winding Topology

### Definition and Counter-Winding

A **bifilar Rodin coil** comprises two conductors wound simultaneously:

- **Winding A**: follows the 150° skip pattern with initial phase \(\phi_0 = 0\)
- **Winding B**: follows the same pattern but with an angular phase offset of **10°** (\(\approx\)1 pole at 12-pole resolution) and wound in the **counter-rotational direction** ([Soul-Guidance — Rodin Torus](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/237fed4a-3b57-45d0-8ae7-90a22ec3a972/Rodin.pdf))

The two windings carry currents \(I_A\) and \(I_B\). In a bifilar anti-parallel configuration (\(I_A = -I_B\)):

- The **differential-mode fields cancel** in far-field (like a bifilar wound resistor)
- A **local toroidal flux is retained** from the geometrically displaced paths
- The **shear fields between adjacent wires** create the characteristic Rodin "crossover" field pattern

The mutual inductance between the two windings is ([MIT — Mutual Inductance](https://web.mit.edu/viz/EM/visualizations/coursenotes/modules/inductance.pdf)):

\[
M_{AB} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \oint_A \oint_B \frac{d\vec{l}_A \cdot d\vec{l}_B}{|\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B|}
\]

(Neumann formula). For the bifilar Rodin geometry, this integral must be evaluated numerically over the discrete conductor paths.

### Total Inductance in Bifilar Configuration

Depending on how windings are connected:

| Connection | Effective Inductance |
|---|---|
| Series aiding (\(I_A = I_B\)) | \(L_{total} = L_A + L_B + 2M\) |
| Series opposing (\(I_A = -I_B\)) | \(L_{total} = L_A + L_B - 2M\) |
| Anti-parallel bifilar | \(L_{total} \to 0\) when \(L_A = L_B = M\) |

The anti-parallel case gives near-zero net inductance — making the coil appear **resistive** across a broad frequency range, with the magnetic field energy stored in the geometric shear between the two paths.

## Part IV — Magnetic Field Mathematics

### Ampère's Law: Ideal Toroid Interior

For an ideal closely-wound toroid with \(N\) total turns, the B-field **inside** the core at radius \(r\) from the central axis ([Physics LibreTexts — Solenoids and Toroids](https://phys.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/University_Physics/University_Physics_(OpenStax)/University_Physics_II_-_Thermodynamics_Electricity_and_Magnetism_(OpenStax)/12:_Sources_of_Magnetic_Fields/12.07:_Solenoids_and_Toroids)):

\[
\boxed{B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}} \quad a_{inner} \le r \le b_{outer}
\]

B-field **outside** the toroid (exterior and center hole):

\[
B = 0 \quad \text{(ideal toroid, no net enclosed current)}
\]

The non-uniform radial distribution means the **average B** over the core cross section is:

\[
\bar{B} = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{\ln(b/a)}{b - a}
\]

### Biot-Savart Law: Numerical Field Calculation

For a discretized wire path with current \(I\), the field at observation point \(\vec{r}\) is ([OSTI — Computation of Biot-Savart](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1817216)):

\[
\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \sum_{k} \frac{d\vec{l}_k \times (\vec{r} - \vec{r}_k)}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_k|^3}
\]

For the full bifilar Rodin coil with two conductors:

\[
\vec{B}_{total} = \vec{B}_A(I_A) + \vec{B}_B(I_B)
\]

The [MagnetiCalc GitHub library](https://github.com/shredEngineer/MagnetiCalc) provides a direct numerical Biot-Savart implementation that accepts arbitrary wire paths and is suitable for this geometry:

\[
A(\vec{x}) = I \cdot \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \sum_{\vec{x}'} \mu_r(\vec{x}) \cdot \frac{\vec{l}(\vec{x}')}{|\vec{x} - \vec{x}'|}
\qquad
\vec{B}(\vec{x}) = I \cdot \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \sum_{\vec{x}'} \mu_r(\vec{x}) \cdot \frac{\vec{l}(\vec{x}') \times (\vec{x} - \vec{x}')}{|\vec{x} - \vec{x}'|^3}
\]

### Magnetic Vector Potential

Alternatively, compute the vector potential first ([CERN Poster — Toroidal Vector Potential](https://indico.cern.ch/event/763185/contributions/3415957/attachments/1911642/3158985/Poster_presentation.pdf)):

\[
\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \oint \frac{d\vec{l}'}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}
\]

then recover B:

\[
\vec{B} = \nabla \times \vec{A}
\]

This approach is especially important for the Rodin toroid because **a toroidal coil produces no exterior B-field but has a non-zero exterior vector potential** — the Aharonov-Bohm vector potential — which means the electromagnetic effect of the coil extends beyond where B = 0 ([SciRP — Pulsed Vector Magnetic Potential](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=24654)).

### Off-Axis Field via Elliptic Integrals

For a single circular current loop of radius \(R\) at a point \((\rho, z)\) in cylindrical coordinates, the exact solution uses complete elliptic integrals \(K(k)\) and \(E(k)\) ([Enthought Mayavi — Magnetic Field Example](http://docs.enthought.com/mayavi/mayavi/auto/example_magnetic_field.html)):

\[
k^2 = \frac{4R\rho}{(R+\rho)^2 + z^2}
\]

\[
B_z = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{1}{\sqrt{(R+\rho)^2+z^2}} \left[ K(k) + E(k)\frac{R^2 - \rho^2 - z^2}{(R-\rho)^2 + z^2} \right]
\]

\[
B_\rho = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{z}{\rho\sqrt{(R+\rho)^2+z^2}} \left[ -K(k) + E(k)\frac{R^2 + \rho^2 + z^2}{(R-\rho)^2 + z^2} \right]
\]

The total coil field is a superposition over all winding segments. This is the analytical reference for validating the numerical Biot-Savart integration.

## Part V — Self-Inductance and Impedance

### Toroid Self-Inductance

For a rectangular-cross-section toroid with inner radius \(a\), outer radius \(b\), height \(h\), and \(N\) turns ([Physics LibreTexts — Self-Inductance](https://phys.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/University_Physics/University_Physics_(OpenStax)/University_Physics_II_-_Thermodynamics_Electricity_and_Magnetism_(OpenStax)/14:_Inductance/14.03:_Self-Inductance_and_Inductors); [Encyclopedia Magnetica — Toroidal Coil Inductance](https://www.e-magnetica.pl/doku.php/calculator/toroidal_coil_inductance)):

\[
\boxed{L = \frac{\mu_r \mu_0 N^2 h}{2\pi} \ln\frac{b}{a}}
\]

For a circular cross-section toroid (minor radius \(a\), major radius \(R\)):

\[
L = \mu_r \mu_0 N^2 \left(R - \sqrt{R^2 - a^2}\right)
\]

Single-layer correction includes the Lorenz terms \(G\) and \(H\) accounting for wire pitch and diameter ([Encyclopedia Magnetica](https://www.e-magnetica.pl/doku.php/calculator/toroidal_coil_inductance)):

\[
G = \frac{5}{4} - \ln\left(2\frac{p}{c}\right), \quad H \approx 0.33790 - 0.43478 N^{-0.8} + \frac{0.096876}{N^2}
\]

### AC Impedance Model

The complex impedance of the coil at angular frequency \(\omega = 2\pi f\):

\[
Z(\omega) = R_{AC}(f) + j\omega L(f)
\]

where \(L(f)\) decreases slightly with frequency due to flux redistribution (skin effect in core), and \(R_{AC}(f)\) increases via the mechanisms below.

### Skin Depth

The **skin depth** — depth into which current penetrates at frequency \(f\) — for copper conductor ([Thompson RD — High Frequency Losses](https://www.thompsonrd.com/Teaching/Power-Electronics/NOTES%2030E%20HIGH%20FREQ%20LOSSES%20IN%20MAGNETICS.pdf)):

\[
\delta = \sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\pi f \mu_0 \mu_r}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi f \mu \sigma}}
\]

For copper at 25°C (\(\rho = 1.68 \times 10^{-8}\) Ω·m):

| Frequency | Skin Depth |
|---|---|
| 60 Hz | ~8.5 mm |
| 1 kHz | ~2.1 mm |
| 10 kHz | ~0.66 mm |
| 100 kHz | ~0.21 mm |
| 1 MHz | ~0.066 mm |

Wire becomes **effectively thinner** with rising frequency. The critical frequency at which skin effect begins to dominate (wire radius \(r_s\)):

\[
f_{critical} = \frac{1}{2\pi \mu \sigma r_s^2}
\]

### AC Resistance: Skin Effect (Dowell Method)

Define the normalized wire radius \(\xi = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2} \frac{d}{\delta}\) where \(d\) is wire diameter. The AC resistance ratio for a single winding layer ([Thompson RD](https://www.thompsonrd.com/Teaching/Power-Electronics/NOTES%2030E%20HIGH%20FREQ%20LOSSES%20IN%20MAGNETICS.pdf)):

\[
\frac{R_{AC}}{R_{DC}} \approx \xi \frac{\sinh\xi + \sin\xi}{\cosh\xi - \cos\xi} \approx \begin{cases} 1 + \frac{\xi^4}{48} & \xi \ll 1 \\ \frac{\xi}{\sqrt{2}} & \xi \gg 1 \end{cases}
\]

### Proximity Effect

For \(M\) winding layers, the **proximity effect** multiplies losses. The Dowell factor is ([Thompson RD](https://www.thompsonrd.com/Teaching/Power-Electronics/NOTES%2030E%20HIGH%20FREQ%20LOSSES%20IN%20MAGNETICS.pdf)):

\[
F_R = \xi \frac{\sinh\xi + \sin\xi}{\cosh\xi - \cos\xi} + \frac{2(M^2-1)}{3} \xi \frac{\sinh\xi - \sin\xi}{\cosh\xi + \cos\xi}
\]

For the bifilar Rodin coil, **proximity effect is enhanced** because the two counter-wound conductors lie adjacent. When currents flow in opposite directions, eddy currents concentrate on the inner-facing surface of each wire, further raising effective resistance ([Altair Flux — Meshed Coil Regions](https://help.altair.com/flux/Flux/Help/english/UserGuide/English/topics/CoilsInFluxMeshedCoilRegions.htm)).

Total coil AC resistance:

\[
R_{coil}(f) = R_{skin}(f) + R_{prox}(f)
\]

### PWM Harmonic Analysis

A PWM signal with fundamental frequency \(f_0\) and duty cycle \(D\) contains harmonics:

\[
v(t) = D V_{dc} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2 V_{dc}}{n\pi} \sin(n\pi D) \cos(2\pi n f_0 t)
\]

Each harmonic at frequency \(n f_0\) experiences:

- Skin depth \(\delta_n = \delta_1 / \sqrt{n}\)
- Resistance \(R_{AC,n} \propto \sqrt{n}\) (at high frequency limit)
- Proximity loss \(\propto n^2\) for eddy-current dominated regime

Total copper loss under PWM:

\[
P_{Cu} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DC} \cdot F_H, \quad F_H = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \hat{I}_n^2 F_R(n f_0) \Big/ \hat{I}_1^2
\]

where \(F_H\) can be **orders of magnitude larger** than 1 when there are many winding layers and harmonics fall near critical frequencies ([Thompson RD](https://www.thompsonrd.com/Teaching/Power-Electronics/NOTES%2030E%20HIGH%20FREQ%20LOSSES%20IN%20MAGNETICS.pdf)).

## Part VII — Voltage, Current, and Wattage Relationships

### Circuit Equations

Under sinusoidal excitation at frequency \(f\), voltage \(V\), the coil behaves as:

\[
V = I \cdot Z(\omega) = I \cdot \sqrt{R_{AC}^2 + (\omega L)^2}
\]

Current magnitude:

\[
I = \frac{V}{|Z|} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{R_{AC}^2 + \omega^2 L^2}}
\]

Peak magnetic flux density (from Faraday's law):

\[
B_{max} = \frac{V_{rms}}{4.44 \cdot f \cdot N \cdot A_e}
\]

where \(A_e = \frac{h(b-a)}{2}\) is the effective core cross-section area. This is the **fundamental design equation** linking your controllable inputs (V, f) to field strength.

### Power Budget

True power (watts) dissipated in coil:

\[
P = I^2 R_{AC}(f) = V^2 \frac{R_{AC}}{R_{AC}^2 + \omega^2 L^2}
\]

Reactive power (VAR) stored and returned each cycle:

\[
Q = I^2 \omega L = V^2 \frac{\omega L}{R_{AC}^2 + \omega^2 L^2}
\]

Apparent power (VA):

\[
S = V \cdot I = I^2 |Z|
\]

Power factor: \(\cos\phi = R_{AC} / |Z|\). At high frequencies the coil becomes highly **reactive** (power factor → 0), meaning most wattage is circulating and little heats the coil.

### Steinmetz Core Loss Model

If a magnetic core material is used, total power loss (Steinmetz equation):

\[
P_{core} = k_h f B_{max}^n + k_e f^2 B_{max}^2 \quad [\text{W/m}^3]
\]

| Term | Name | Frequency Scaling |
|---|---|---|
| \(k_h f B^n\) | Hysteresis loss | Linear with \(f\) |
| \(k_e f^2 B^2\) | Classical eddy current | Quadratic with \(f\) |
| Ferrite eddy current | Anomalous | \(\propto f^4\) in some materials |

For air-core Rodin coils, \(k_h = k_e = 0\) and all loss is copper loss. This is one reason researchers use air-core Rodin geometries — to eliminate core saturation and hysteresis.

## Part VIII — Digital Twin State Equations

### State Variable Model

The digital twin tracks these coupled state variables at each simulation timestep:

| State Variable | Symbol | Units | Governing Equation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current through A | \(I_A\) | A | \(V_A = L_A \dot{I}_A + M \dot{I}_B + R_A I_A\) |
| Current through B | \(I_B\) | A | \(V_B = L_B \dot{I}_B + M \dot{I}_A + R_B I_B\) |
| Magnetic flux A | \(\Phi_A\) | Wb | \(\Phi_A = L_A I_A + M I_B\) |
| Magnetic flux B | \(\Phi_B\) | Wb | \(\Phi_B = L_B I_B + M I_A\) |
| B-field at sample point | \(\vec{B}(\vec{r})\) | T | Biot-Savart (numerical) |

In matrix form:

\[
\begin{pmatrix} V_A \\ V_B \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} L_A & M \\ M & L_B \end{pmatrix} \frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix} I_A \\ I_B \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} R_A(f) & 0 \\ 0 & R_B(f) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} I_A \\ I_B \end{pmatrix}
\]

### Frequency, PWM, and Voltage Control Mapping

For your adjustable parameters, the digital twin maps:

**Frequency \(f\):**
\[
\delta(f) = \sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\pi f \mu_0}}, \quad R_{AC}(f) = R_{DC} \cdot F_R\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\frac{d}{\delta(f)}\right), \quad B_{max}(f) = \frac{V}{4.44 f N A_e}
\]

**Voltage \(V\):**
\[
I = \frac{V}{|Z(f)|}, \quad B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}, \quad P = \frac{V^2 R_{AC}}{R_{AC}^2 + \omega^2 L^2}
\]

**PWM Duty Cycle \(D\) and Switching Frequency \(f_{sw}\):**
\[
V_{eff} = D \cdot V_{dc}, \quad I_{n,peak} = \frac{2 V_{dc}}{n\pi |Z(n f_{sw})|}|\sin(n\pi D)|
\]
\[
B_{fundamental} = \frac{\mu_0 N I_1}{2\pi r}, \quad B_{total} = \sqrt{\sum_n B_n^2} \text{ (RMS)}
\]

**Wattage \(P_{input}\):**

Given input power budget, the achievable B field is:

\[
B_{max} = \frac{\mu_0 N}{2\pi r_{mean}} \sqrt{\frac{P_{input}}{R_{AC}(f)}}
\]

This is the central design equation for optimizing field strength per watt.

## Part IX — Recommended Digital Twin Architecture

### Numerical Pipeline for Each Timestep

```
1. INPUT:  V(t), f(t), D(t)  [from UI sliders]
2. COMPUTE:
   a. ω = 2πf
   b. δ(f) = sqrt(ρ / πfμ₀)         [skin depth]
   c. R_AC(f) = R_DC · F_R(d/δ)     [Dowell method]
   d. Z = R_AC + jωL                 [impedance]
   e. I_A = V·D / |Z|               [RMS current, winding A]
   f. I_B = -I_A                    [anti-parallel bifilar]
3. B-FIELD:
   a. For each observation point r:
      B(r) = μ₀NI / 2πr            [inside core, fast path]
      OR
      B(r) = Σ μ₀I/(4π) · (dl×R̂/R²) [Biot-Savart, 3D path]
4. OUTPUT:
   - B(x,y,z) field volume array
   - Power: P = I²·R_AC
   - Reactive power: Q = I²·ωL
   - Flux density B_max = V/4.44fNA_e
```
